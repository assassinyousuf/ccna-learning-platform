# Assessment & Review: Volume 1 Chapter 3 - Chapter 3: Cables, connectors, and ports

### Question 1: What is the standard maximum cable length of a copper UTP cable?
- A. 1 kilometer
- B. 100 meters
- C. 100 feet
- D. 1000 feet

> **Official Correct Answer**: **B** — B: 100 meters  
> **Technical Explanation**: The standard maximum length for copper unshielded twisted pair (UTP) cables in an Ethernet network is 100 meters (328 feet). Attempts to use UTP cables longer than 100 meters can result in signal attenuation and decreased performance.


---

### Question 2: Which technology allows a device to automatically adjust which pin pairs it uses to transmit and receive data in a copper UTP cable?
- A. Crossover
- B. Straight-through
- C. Auto MDI-X
- D. Tx/Rx

> **Official Correct Answer**: **C** — C: Auto MDI-X  
> **Technical Explanation**: Automatic Medium-Dependent Interface Crossover (Auto MDI-X) is a technology that allows devices to automatically adjust their pin configurations without needing specific crossover or straight-through cables.


---

### Question 3: Which of the following cable types supports connections over the greatest distances?
- A. Copper UTP
- B. Multimode fiber
- C. Single-mode fiber

> **Official Correct Answer**: **A** —   
> **Technical Explanation**: ## C: Single-mode fiber Single-mode fiber optic cables are designed for long-distance communication, often over many kilometers. They have a smaller core diameter than multimode fiber, allowing only one mode of light to travel along the cable. This reduces signal attenuation and allows data to travel longer distances.

### Question 4: How many bits can a 1 Gbps interface transmit per second?
- A. 1000 bps
- B. 1,000,000 bps
- C. 1,000,000,000 bps
- D. 1,000,000,000,000 bps

> **Official Correct Answer**: **C** — C: 1,000,000,000 bps  
> **Technical Explanation**: The prefix “giga” means “billion”, so a 1 Gbps (gigabit per second) interface can transmit 1,000,000,000 (one billion) bits per second.


---

### Question 5: Which of the following is equivalent to 1 Mbps?
- A. 1000 kbps
- B. 1024 kbps
- C. 1000 bps
- D. 1024 bps

> **Official Correct Answer**: **A** — A: 1000 kbps  
> **Technical Explanation**: The term Mbps stands for megabits per second, and kbps stands for kilobits per second. The prefix “mega” means “million” and the prefix “kilo” means “thousand”, so 1 Mbps is equivalent to 1000 kbps.


---

### Question 6: Which of the following is NOT an advantage of fiberoptic cables over copper UTP cables?
- A. Less vulnerable to EMI
- B. Lower cost
- B. Greater maximum distances
- B. Less security risk

> **Official Correct Answer**: **B** — B: Lower cost  
> **Technical Explanation**: The main advantages of fiber-optic cables over copper UTP cables include being less vulnerable to electromagnetic interference (EMI), supporting greater maximum transmission distances, and posing less security risk due to leaked signals. However, fiber-optic connections are generally more expensive due to both the cables and the small form-factor pluggable (SFP) transceivers.


---

### Question 7: What is the minimum copper UTP cable standard that supports 1 Gbps Ethernet?
- A. Cat 6a
- B. Cat 5e
- C. Cat 5
- D. Cat 3

> **Official Correct Answer**: **B** — B: Cat 5e  
> **Technical Explanation**: While the original Cat5 standard could theoretically support gigabit speeds, Cat5e is specifically designed to support 1 Gbps and is generally considered the minimum requirement for Gigabit Ethernet.
